How a card turns into an idea

MiniaturesA matchbox dioramaAs a gift

Imagine a gig small enough to fit in your pocket. Fold a scrap of paper into a zigzag crowd, stand it inside a matchbox and write your friend's name on the ticket. You can stop at any point along the card's path.

If you deal two cards, try borrowing one detail from each. With Maps and Photography, take pictures of three doorways on your street and use them as landmarks on a hand-drawn map.

Three creative prompts to try now

Here are three ways to use ideas from the deck. Pick the one you can start with what's in front of you.

Draw a smell map

Mark your front door with a dot. Map the nearest three smells you remember, with a different symbol for each. Fresh toast might be a little brown square.

Write the lost-key story

"My spare key came home wet." Where did it go? Answer in six words. Have ten minutes? Write the first 100 words in the key's voice.

Photograph a reflection

Find a shiny spoon. Move it until a window fits inside the curve, then take the picture with the handle still in the frame.

Good to know

Is it free?

Yes. There's no account, no sign-up and nothing to install. On an iPhone you can add it to your Home Screen from Safari's Share menu.

Does it use AI?

No AI chooses or writes your prompt. Every card comes from a fixed list of about 260 categories written in advance, and your device's random generator picks from it. The ideas are for you to make, not prompts for an AI image or chat tool.

Do I need to be good at art?

No. Most cards work with a pen, your phone camera or things already in your room. Stop at the category, or go deeper until it feels small enough to start.

What if I don't like my card?

Pick again for a fresh hand, or step back a layer and go deeper for a different detail. To choose a card's category yourself, hold the card or press Enter on it.
