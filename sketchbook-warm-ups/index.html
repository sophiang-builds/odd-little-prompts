Deal a prompt

Three five-minute sketchbook warm-ups

Put a mug, a key or a pair of scissors next to your sketchbook. No scissors handy? A fork or a chair works too. Pick one warm-up and give it five minutes. If you want to fill a page, use the same object for all three.

1. Draw the gaps 5 minutes

Spend 30 seconds looking only for empty spaces: the hole in a mug's handle, the finger holes in scissors, the wedge of air between the blades. For the next four minutes, draw only those shapes. In the last 30 seconds, add the smallest mark that makes the object recognisable, and leave the rest of the outline open.

2. Change the scale 5 minutes

Draw a rectangle the size of a postage stamp and fit the whole object inside it. In a second rectangle, zoom in until one edge of the object crosses the entire frame. Spend the last minute adding a person no bigger than a grain of rice. Where would they stand?

3. Give it a job 5 minutes

Imagine your object works the night shift. Draw it at the start of its shift, halfway through and just before it goes home, changing its angle in each little scene. Add one prop that tells us what the job is, and stop when someone could guess it without a caption.

Stuck for a job? Try a mug working the night shift at a lost-and-found desk. Draw its handle as one big listening ear, then add the tiny mitten someone has come to claim.

Make another warm-up

Tomorrow, repeat one exercise with something soft, like a sock or a folded scarf. Or let the deck choose: it can deal you one specific thing to draw, or leave the category open and sketch whatever comes up.
